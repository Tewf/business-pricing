# Willingness to pay: measuring it before a sale, and what a self-designed good adds

A first-pass multivocal review, read on 2026-11-04. It asks how willingness to pay is measured before any sale, how far a stated answer misses a paid one, what a self-designed good adds, and where law and ethics stop two prices. It ends on a named baseline.

- It holds 39 sources: 30 formal and 9 grey, three of the grey below the threshold and serving as examples of practice only.
- Grey: 3 tier 1, 2 tier 2 and 4 tier 3. No controlled study of a waiting list was found.
- Formal: 19 measured, 4 supported with caveat, 4 convention and 3 convention argued from a model. Most numbers are read from abstracts and marked (secondary).

## Named baseline

- **B1, a stated range checked against a paid measure on the same good.**
- A sample answers the Van Westendorp prices and a Gabor-Granger ladder; a second answers by a BDM bid or a pre-order with a deposit.
- The hypothetical bias is reported as a ratio, and the paid figure is the one that counts ([baseline](baseline.md)).
