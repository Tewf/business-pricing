# Position

Step 4 of the review. What is known, what was not found, and what this repository asks. Each "not found" is a statement about these searches, not about the world. Terms are in [frame](frame.md).

**Shape:** [position](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/c43f2770000a9af5c2aa3cbc6f997da7a7e13b34/shapes/position.md)

## Known

- A stated willingness to pay overstates a paid one: by 21% on average for consumer goods (Schmidt 2020), by a median ratio of 1.35 (Murphy 2005).
- Indirect methods do not beat direct ones on accuracy (Schmidt 2020).
- BDM and incentive-aligned conjoint pass tests against real purchases; open-ended and conjoint answers may still rank prices well (Miller 2011).
- A self-designed good was bid up by 37% to 92% over the same good off the shelf, by students, in BDM (Franke 2010).
- The premium is carried by a feeling of accomplishment, grows with the fit of the design and the freedom given, and needs a finished task (Franke 2010; Norton 2012).
- The meter is hypothetical and biased, and its results were close to BDM in one comparison (Kloss 2016).
- A personalised price is lawful in the EU when disclosed and when data protection law is met (EU 2019 Directive; EU 2016 GDPR; Borgesius 2017).

## Not found in these searches

- A study of a waiting list or a sign-up as a predictor of purchase (F18, F19, F20).
- A premium for self-design measured on buyers in a real sale, or outside a classroom (F14, F15, F17).
- A comparison of the Van Westendorp range, the Gabor-Granger curve and a paid pre-order on the same good (F2, F11, F13).
- The law of a member state in which the maintainer sells (not searched).
- An adoption count for the meter or the curve (F2, F11).

## The questions

| Question | Answer from the evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Which measure predicts what people pay? | BDM and incentive-aligned conjoint, against real purchases; a stated answer misses by about 21% on consumer goods, with a long tail | measured |
| Q2 How large is the premium, and when does it vanish? | +37% to +92% in BDM bids by students, about +100% on watches in one abstract; it vanishes when the task is unfinished and shrinks when the design fits poorly | measured |
| Q3 What does a waiting list say about demand? | no study found; the nearest, stated intention, beats extrapolation for existing products; a free name is a stated intention at no cost | not found; measured for intentions |
| Q4 Where do law and ethics stop two prices? | the price must be disclosed as personalised, and a solely automated decision with significant effect needs a contract, a law or explicit consent | convention |
| Q5 How does a first number act? | arbitrary anchors moved valuations; the result calls for caution | measured |

## What this repository asks

- Does a premium for self-design measured by students in BDM hold for buyers in a real sale, and at what price?
- By how much does the Van Westendorp range of one good miss a paid pre-order of the same good?
- What must a waiting list record, such as the price shown at sign-up and a small deposit, to predict purchase?

## What the shapes must decide

- The first design document takes B1 and says the good, the paid measure and the stakes ([baseline](baseline.md)).
- It states which law applies to the places the maintainer sells in, found in the texts.
- It sets the number of respondents and the rule for dropping inconsistent answers before any survey.
